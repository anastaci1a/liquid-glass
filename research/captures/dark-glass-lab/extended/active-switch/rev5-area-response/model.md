- The two active switch frames have different thumb sizes relative to the visible track cap. A shared normalized map assumes those geometric differences do not change optical strength. Test that assumption explicitly; do not insert separate fitted coefficients for the two frames.
- Measure A from the existing crowned footprint, and R from the exposed track-cap radius already recorded in each frozen case. Define u=A/(πR²). Uniform video zoom cancels. Fix u0 as the geometric mean of the two measured u values before inspecting target-image errors. This is a canonical pose normalization, not a fitted color or displacement.
- Candidate inverse map: F_k(x)=x+(u/u0)^(-k)(F_0(x)−x), one shared k∈[0,2]. k=0 exactly recovers the accepted map. All source data, silhouette/coverage, lighting, ordinary blur, directional dispersion, shadow and scoring masks remain unchanged. The constant u0 merely defines which pose retains F_0.
- Motivation only: for geometrically similar planar footprints with in-plane scale λ, fixed volume implies height∝λ⁻² and slope∝λ⁻³. In a weak-slope thin-lens approximation at fixed physical backdrop gap, normalized lateral transport scales λ⁻⁴, or area⁻². This motivates k=2 as a comparator. Intermediate k is phenomenological. The inherited folded map is NOT a reconstructed solid, and these dynamic frames need not conserve volume or be exact similarities.
- R is an observed geometric proxy for a fixed UI scale, not a recovered camera calibration. Track fitting, actual press state, slight shape changes and source reconstruction remain confounds. No claim that a lower error identifies Apple's volume/depth law. If supported, sensitivity to independently measured pose uncertainty and a further state/background remain necessary.
