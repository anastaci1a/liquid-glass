APPLE LIQUID GLASS — FOCUSED OPTICS RESEARCH
15 October 2026 · continuation of LIQUID-GLASS-RESEARCH-HANDOFF.md.txt
Tracking: https://github.com/anastaci1a/localica/issues/222

Outcome
• A shallow, smoothly rounded cushion with stronger peripheral optics is a useful design interpretation. It is NOT a reconstructed physical surface. A quiet optical interior does not imply a visibly flattened face or a sharp bevel.
• A newly acquired Apple morph demo supplies an exposed reference background at some locations before glass covers them. This permits a conditional local comparison without reconstructing the source from a different photograph.
• In one selected, label-free patch, modest inverse displacement plus blur explains the image better than either alone. The old Localica crown model overpredicts displacement there; the experimental boundary model is closer but also stronger than the fitted local translation.
• Do not translate this single patch into a universal depth, blur radius, pressure equation, or claim of Apple parity. Multiple parameter combinations fit similarly, and material lighting matters.

Registration and patch selection
• Morph frame 0 exposes the background to the right of the original center circle. Later split states cover it with the right circle. Foreground icons remain excluded.
• Registration masks exclude the union of glass footprints, the Toggle button, and a surrounding margin. Background translation fitted across six frames is below 0.003 decoded pixels; RGB mean absolute exterior residual is 1.10–1.13/255. Subpixel fitted translations below raster precision are not proof of equivalent measurement accuracy.
• This is same-scene background registration, not calibration of an Android source/window transform. #223 still requires its own native zero-power tests.
• Chosen patch: center (423,60), 21×21 pixels. Approximate right-circle center (429,114), radius 83. The patch is above the eraser glyph; visual inspection checked its footprint.
• Training frame: zero-based 65, PTS 1.133333 s.
• Repeated-frame checks: 80 / 1.400000 s; 95 / 1.666667 s; 225 / 4.066667 s; 240 / 4.350000 s; 255 / 4.616667 s.
• These repeats reuse the same background and nearly the same presentation. They test repeatability, not independent-scene generalization or known held/released states.

Conditional local fit
• Model: Gaussian-preblur the exposed source; sample a translated patch; fit nonnegative per-channel gain (0–2) and offset. Calculations use decoded 8-bit RGB values, not radiometrically calibrated linear light.
• Grid: sigma 0–6 px, dx/dy −5…5 px, all in 0.25 px steps. Spatial/blur parameters train on frame 65 only. Gain/offset refit on each comparison frame.
• Best local fit: sigma 3 px; inverse lookup offset (0,+2.5) px. This says the output patch samples below its display coordinate; it is NOT a claim that the displayed feature moves downward.
• Fits within +0.15 RGB RMSE of the minimum span sigma 1.5–4.5, dx −1…+1.25, dy +1…+3.75 px. This is a sensitivity region, not a statistical confidence interval.
• Mean RGB RMSE over the five non-training frames (0–255 units):
  - Identity geometry, no blur, fitted tone: 3.260
  - Independently optimized blur only: 2.883
  - Independently optimized translation only: 2.794
  - Joint blur + translation: 2.517
• Allowing a smooth additive illumination plane changes the best sigma from 3 to 2 px and dx from 0 to −0.5 px; dy remains +2.5 px. The repeated-frame RMSE becomes 1.872, versus 2.221 for independently optimized blur with no shift under the same illumination allowance.
• Thus tone/lighting is a meaningful confound. The fit supports a useful local optical comparison; it does not determine a unique curvature or blur function.
• Blue is nearly uninformative in this pale patch. An unconstrained early fit used negative blue gain; that fit was rejected. Final fits prohibit inversion. Do not interpret a gain at zero as proof that Apple removes that channel.

Fixed Localica model comparison
• Compare the same approximate 83px-radius circle and full per-pixel patch mapping. Fit blur on frame 65 separately for each fixed optical model; refit per-channel tone per frame. These are Python evaluations of source formulas, NOT Android screenshots or end-to-end renderer tests.
• Existing crown at depth 1.35 (local LensGeometry.kt): patch-center inverse offset (+1.05,+9.44) px; fitted sigma 4.75; repeated-frame RMSE 3.223.
• PR #240 boundary default: patch-center inverse offset (+0.61,+5.52) px; fitted sigma 3.5; repeated-frame RMSE 2.755.
• Change the assumed center x/y and radius independently by ±2 px: crown vertical prediction 8.09–11.06 px; boundary 4.64–6.14 px. This sensitivity does not account for every possible silhouette or material-state error.
• The boundary candidate improves this comparison, but its local lookup is still stronger than the translated-patch fit. It was calibrated from a different clear-slider scene; scaling it directly to every regular/frosted circle is an unverified hypothesis.
• Comparing raw fit errors is descriptive, not a statistical model-selection result: the flexible local translation has more freedom, spatial lighting is simplified, neighboring pixels/frames are correlated, and the true rendering order is unknown.

Rejected inferences / checks
• Dense high-correlation matches alone are not reliable evidence. Many exploratory patches hit the maximum blur search range, followed a single ridge, approached a glyph, or varied substantially across repeats. Retain their raw output for audit, but do not fit a global radial field from them.
• Do not call a screenshot's silhouette a recovered z-depth. Outline geometry, refraction, blur, and photometry are not interchangeable measurements.
• Do not infer a flat face from a registered central background. Likewise, stronger uniform central zoom is not evidence of a better rounded lens.
• Do not infer pressure or latency from material brightness onset without independent input information.
• The analysis recovers identity and a synthetic known sigma=2, dx=−1, dy=+2.5 model under positive RGB gain/offset to numerical precision (<0.00001 RGB RMSE). This checks the fit's coordinate sign and arithmetic, not its physical interpretation.

Ranked next implementation experiments — keep isolated
1. Keep source registration and optical power independently testable. Run zero-power asymmetric grids through actual transformed/pressed/morphing bounds on both rendering paths before tuning the optical model. PR #240's adapter checks do not replace all #223 ancestor/deformation cases.
2. Compare independent core and shoulder optical controls over matched circle/capsule/panel scenes. Preserve a smooth derivative through the shoulder; avoid a visible flat-center/bevel boundary. Sweep shoulder strength on a separate calibration scene, then freeze it for this newly acquired regular-circle patch and the earlier clear-slider held-outs. Do not raise global depth as the default remedy.
3. Calibrate clear and regular material scattering/photometry separately. Keep the geometric inverse map observable before adding blur and illumination. Evaluate the dim/bright background cases too; apparent success on a pale patch is insufficient.
4. Capture rest/contact/held/release with an independent input cue and registered textured backing. Only then fit pressure-dependent curvature and touch-origin lighting. Keep the user's flatten-while-held / rebound-on-release preference distinct from measured Apple behavior.
5. Profile the surviving candidate on native Android geometry at odd/fractional sizes and through interrupted morphs. No production replacement or performance claim follows from this research script alone.
