# Fixed-height curvature redistribution

Status: proposed physical surface, not fitted refraction. The unchanged harmonic construction supplies both intersections and normals.

Let the transverse and longitudinal height fields be:

$$
h_1=d(1-\rho^3),\qquad h_2=c\left(1-\frac{x^2}{a^2}\right),\qquad h=\frac{h_1h_2}{h_1+h_2}.
$$

The original rounded-rectangle coordinate and footprint remain fixed. Positive concave inputs and the increasing concave harmonic combination retain the existing concavity certificate.

At the center:

$$
H=\frac{dc}{d+c},\qquad
\left.\frac{\partial^2h}{\partial x^2}\right|_{(0,0)}=-\frac{2c}{a^2}\left(\frac{d}{d+c}\right)^2.
$$

The two parameter pairs preserve height while changing curvature:

$$
(d_0,c_0)=(0.15,0.4),\qquad(d_1,c_1)=(0.24,0.2),\qquad H_0=H_1=\frac{6}{55}.
$$

The candidate doubles central longitudinal curvature magnitude. Transverse slope also changes; this is not an isolated horizontal displacement or a promise of improved fidelity.

The existing conservative slope certificate remains below the first-exit critical slope:

$$
\|\nabla h\|\leq\max\left(\frac{3d}{r},\frac{2c}{a}\right)\approx0.717
<\frac{1}{\sqrt{n^2-1}}\approx0.894,\qquad n=1.5.
$$

This bound covers the first vertical internal branch only. Complete numerical paths, source support and observed feature correspondence still require their declared checks.

The projected crease stays unresolved. No zero-height pole is converted into a transmitting path. Maximum thickness preservation does not imply equal image magnification or equal material response.
